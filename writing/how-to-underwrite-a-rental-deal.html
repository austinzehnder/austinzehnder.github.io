How to underwrite a rental deal in 15 minutes

Austin Zehnder · October 30, 2026

Here is the honest truth I have learned after years of underwriting homes: most rental deals are not lost on a fancy model. They are lost on one optimistic assumption, copied across every line. So before I spend real time on a property, I run this quick 15 minute check. If it fails here, I move on. No drama, no hoping the market bails me out.

1. Rent: use the conservative number

Start with what similar homes actually rented for, not what the listing says they could rent for. If the deal only works at the very top of the range, it does not really work yet. I would rather be pleasantly surprised than quietly underwater.

2. Taxes and insurance: verify, do not guess

These two lines move more than most buyers expect, and they move every year. Pull the actual tax bill and get a real insurance quote before you trust any spreadsheet, including mine.

3. Maintenance and capex: budget for reality

A home does not stay new just because you just bought it. I set aside a fixed slice for ongoing maintenance, plus a separate reserve for the big stuff: roof, HVAC, water heater. Those bills are not surprises. They are just scheduled, badly.

4. Vacancy: price in the gap

Even a great tenant leaves eventually. Life happens. So I underwrite a vacancy allowance up front, so a normal turnover does not turn a good year into a bad one.

5. Cash flow after everything

Only now do I subtract debt service. If monthly cash flow is thin after rent minus taxes, insurance, maintenance, capex, vacancy, and management, I walk away or renegotiate. Hoping is not a strategy.

If a deal passes all five, then it earns a deeper look. That is where a full model helps. The Rental Deal Analyzer I built for Zehndra uses the same discipline, just with scenarios, a 10 year view, and a scorecard instead of a gut feeling.
