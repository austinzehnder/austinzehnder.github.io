How to underwrite a rental deal in 15 minutes

By Austin Zehnder · San Francisco finance & real estate professional

Most rental deals are not lost on a fancy model. They are lost on one optimistic assumption, repeated across every line. This is the 15-minute check I use before spending any more time on a property.

1. Rent — use the conservative number

Start with what comparable homes actually rented for, not the listing's pro forma. If the top of the range is the only way the deal works, it does not work yet.

2. Taxes and insurance — verify, don't estimate

These two lines move more than most buyers expect, and they move every year. Pull the actual tax bill and a real insurance quote before you trust a spreadsheet.

3. Maintenance and capex — budget for reality

A home does not stay new because you bought it recently. Set aside a fixed percentage for ongoing maintenance, plus a separate reserve for roofs, HVAC, and other big-ticket items.

4. Vacancy — price in the gap

Even a great tenant leaves eventually. Underwrite a vacancy allowance up front, so a normal turnover does not turn a good year into a bad one.

5. Cash flow after everything

Only now subtract debt service. If monthly cash flow is thin after rent minus taxes, insurance, maintenance, capex, vacancy, and management, walk away or renegotiate — do not hope the market saves it.

If a deal passes all five, it earns a deeper look. That is where a full model, like the Rental Deal Analyzer I built for Zehndra, earns its keep: same discipline, just with scenarios, a 10-year view, and a scorecard instead of a gut feeling.
